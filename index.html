About the workshop

Two island ecologies, one conversation

Taiwan and Japan share mountains that rise steeply from warm seas, monsoon-driven seasons, and biotas shaped by the same currents and flyways. They also share a generation of ecologists asking how organisms adapt, cooperate, and shift their ranges as the climate warms.

This workshop brings together researchers from both countries for three days of talks, posters, and discussion across behavioral ecology, evolutionary genomics, macroecology, and conservation. The aim is simple: to turn parallel research programs into shared ones, and to seed collaborations that outlast the meeting.

Sessions are designed to leave room for conversation. Alongside keynote and contributed talks, the program includes a poster session, an early-career mixer, and an optional field excursion to one of the mountain sites that shape ecology on both sides of the strait.

Scientific themes

What we will talk about

Six threads running through both countries' research, chosen because they invite direct comparison across the Taiwan-Japan gradient.

01

Behavioral & social evolution

Cooperation, breeding systems, and social structure, from insects to vertebrates, and the ecological conditions that favor them.

02

Evolutionary genomics

Genomic architecture of adaptation, including structural variants, supergenes, and the genetics of complex traits.

03

Macroecology & biogeography

Elevational and latitudinal gradients, range dynamics, and how diversity is distributed across two mountainous archipelagos.

04

Species on the move

Range shifts, thermal niches, and the conservation challenges of a warming climate for marine and terrestrial species alike.

05

Community & ecosystem ecology

Species interactions, coexistence, and the processes that assemble and maintain communities through time.

06

Theory & methods

Models, simulation, and computational approaches that connect mechanism to pattern across scales.
